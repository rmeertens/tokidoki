Festival & party chants in Japanese: 祭りと掛け声

What people shout together — at festivals, fireworks, games and parties.

  • わっしょい！わっしょい！ — Wasshoi! Wasshoi! Carrying a portable shrine (神輿) through the streets at a festival.
  • たーまやー！ — Ta-ma-ya! Shouted at fireworks — Tamaya was a famous Edo-period fireworks maker.
  • 鬼は外！福は内！ — Demons out! Luck in! Setsubun (3 February): throw roasted beans at someone in a demon mask.
  • かんぱーい！ — Cheers! 乾杯 — literally "dry the cup". Wait for everyone before the first sip.
  • じゃんけんぽん！あいこでしょ！ — Rock, paper, scissors! A tie, so again! Japan's way to settle anything. あいこ = a draw.
  • せーの！ — Ready… go! To lift, jump or say something all together.
  • ばんざーい！ — Banzai! (Ten thousand years!) Hands up three times — for an election win, a wedding, a big success.
  • よっ、日本一！ — Hey — best in Japan! A kabuki-audience cheer, now used to hype a friend.
  • はっけよい、のこった！ — Hakkeyoi — still in! The sumo referee: のこった means the wrestlers are still in the ring.
  • ニッポン、チャチャチャ！ — Nippon, cha-cha-cha! The clap-along chant at Japan's national-team games.
  • ドンマイ！ — Don't mind! Japanese-English for "never mind, it's OK" after a mistake in sports.
  • ファイト、オー！ — Fight — oh! A team's hands-in cheer before a match or school event.

Story: 夏祭り (The summer festival)

八月、町でお祭りがありました。 (In August there was a festival in town.) 男の人たちが、神輿をかついでいます。 (Men are carrying a portable shrine.) みんな: わっしょい！わっしょい！ (Wasshoi! Wasshoi!) ユキとケンは、たこ焼きを買いました。最後の一つを賭けて、じゃんけんです。 (Yuki and Ken bought takoyaki. They played rock-paper-scissors for the last one.) 二人: じゃんけんぽん！あいこでしょ！ (Rock, paper, scissors! A tie, so again!) ケン: 負けた… (I lost…) ユキ: ドンマイ！ (Never mind!) 夜九時、空に大きな花火が上がりました。 (At nine, a huge firework burst in the sky.) みんな: たーまやー！ (Ta-ma-ya!)
