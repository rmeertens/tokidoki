Famous ad slogans in Japanese: 有名なキャッチコピー

Jingles and taglines every Japanese person can finish for you.

  • お、ねだん以上。ニトリ — Oh — more than the price. Nitori. Furniture chain Nitori's jingle. The お、 at the start is both "oh!" and the polite prefix of お値段 (price) — a tiny pun.
  • やめられない、とまらない、かっぱえびせん — Can't quit, can't stop — Kappa Ebisen. Calbee's shrimp crackers, since the 1960s. People quote it about anything addictive.
  • そうだ 京都、行こう。 — I know — let's go to Kyoto. JR Central's travel campaign since 1993. Swap in any place: そうだ 温泉、行こう。
  • インテル、入ってる — Intel — it's inside. Japan's version of "Intel Inside": インテル and 入ってる (hai-tteru) rhyme.
  • 100人乗っても大丈夫！ — Even with 100 people on it, it's fine! Inaba garden sheds: the ad shows a crowd of 100 people standing on the roof.
  • セブン-イレブン、いい気分 — Seven-Eleven — feeling good. The convenience store's classic jingle: イレブン and 気分 (kibun) rhyme.
  • あなたと、コンビに、ファミリーマート — You and me — at the convenience store — FamilyMart. A pun: コンビに means both "as a pair (コンビ)" and "at the コンビニ".
  • ファイト！一発！ — Fight! One shot! Lipovitan D energy drink: two men clinging to a cliff shout it as one pulls the other up.
  • 24時間戦えますか — Can you fight for 24 hours? Regain energy drink, 1989 — the bubble-era salaryman anthem. Quoted today with a sigh.
  • 元気ハツラツ！オロナミンC — Full of beans! Oronamin C. The little brown bottle of fizzy vitamin drink. ハツラツ = lively, bursting with energy.
  • お口の恋人、ロッテ — Your mouth's sweetheart — Lotte. The gum and chocolate maker's slogan for decades.
  • カラダにピース。カルピス — Peace for your body. Calpis. ピース (peace) rhymes with ピス in カルピス.
  • 目のつけどころが、シャープでしょ。 — Our eye for detail is sharp, right? Electronics maker Sharp: 目のつけどころがシャープ "a sharp way of looking at things".
  • おしりだって、洗ってほしい。 — Bottoms want washing too. TOTO's 1982 ad that made the Washlet bidet toilet famous.
  • うまい、やすい、はやい — Tasty, cheap, fast. Yoshinoya's promise for its beef bowls (the order of the three has changed over the years). Now used for anything good and quick.
  • リンゴとハチミツ、とろ〜り溶けてる — Apples and honey, melting in nice and gooey. House Vermont Curry — the mild curry Japanese kids grow up on.
  • ピッカピカの一年生 — Shiny new first-graders. Shogakukan's magazine ads showing kids about to start school. ピカピカ = sparkling new.
  • 亭主元気で留守がいい — Best when the husband's healthy — and out. A 1986 ad for wardrobe insect repellent that became a buzzword for bored housewives.
  • スカッとさわやか、コカ・コーラ — Crisp and refreshing — Coca-Cola. Coke's Japanese slogan from the 1960s–80s. スカッと = the feeling of a fizzy, clean hit.
  • わんぱくでもいい、たくましく育ってほしい — Naughty's fine — just grow up strong. Marudai ham, 1970s: a boy bites into a huge ham on the bone.

Story: コマーシャルの時間 (Commercial break)

日曜日の夜、ケンとユキはテレビを見ています。 (Sunday night. Ken and Yuki are watching TV.) テレビ: やめられない、とまらない… (Can't quit, can't stop…) ケン: かっぱえびせん！ (Kappa Ebisen!) ユキ: ケン、もう一袋食べてるよ。 (Ken, you've already eaten a whole bag.) テレビ: そうだ 京都、行こう。 (I know — let's go to Kyoto.) ケン: そうだ、京都、行こう！ (I know — let's go to Kyoto!) ユキ: え、今から？もう夜の十時だよ。 (What, now? It's already ten at night.) ケン: 大丈夫！24時間戦えますから！ (No problem! I can fight for 24 hours!) ユキ: それ、古いよ… (That one's ancient…)
