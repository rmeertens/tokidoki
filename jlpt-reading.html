JLPT reading: notices, emails & flyers

The practical texts of the JLPT reading section: a notice, an email, a memo or an invitation, where you work out what someone must do or what the writer’s point is — and flyers and price lists, where you find the one option that fits. Our own texts, at the length and in the style of each level, with every answer explained.

Notices, emails & memos 内容理解（短文）

A short practical text and one question: what must you do, by when — or what does the writer most want to say? The answer is usually one sentence; the wrong choices mix up who does what, or skip a ただし.

Information retrieval 情報検索

A flyer, price list or timetable, and someone with a situation: which class fits, how much do they pay, when can they book? Read the question first, then check each option against each condition.

  • “What must you do?” — look for the request: 〜てください, 〜ていただけないでしょうか, and a deadline, 〜までに.

The whole text in English

JLPT reading practice — notices, emails, memos and information retrieval, N5 to N2

Free, no-login practice for the practical texts in the reading section of the Japanese-Language Proficiency Test. In content comprehension, short texts (内容理解・短文) you read a notice, an email, a memo or an invitation and answer one question in Japanese: what does the reader have to do, by when — or what does the writer most want to say? A shopping list, a birthday invitation, a homestay thank-you letter, a shift swap, an interview invitation, a postponed fireworks display, a lease renewal. In information retrieval (情報検索) you scan a flyer, a price list or a set of rules — swimming classes, a café menu, an airport bus timetable, a museum's ticket prices, library loans, bus tours, gym plans, part-time jobs, parcel charges, oversized rubbish, a scholarship, internships, coworking plans, booking a hall — and find the one option that fits someone's situation.

The texts are Tokidoki's own, written at each level's length and style: about 80 characters for an N5 note, spaced between phrases as on the N5 paper; 100–200 for N4 notices and emails; 150–200 for N3 business emails; about 200 for N2 business documents with formal keigo; and 250, 400, 600 and 700 characters for the information texts. After each answer the part of the text it rests on is highlighted and the answer explained — who does what, which sentence after しかし or そこで holds the point, which ただし or ※ line holds the exception — and the whole text can be read in English with its harder words. Once the questions are answered, every word can be tapped for its reading and meaning (and added to your flashcards), and the text can be read sentence by sentence: each sentence with its English and the grammar it uses — お〜ください, ただし, 〜場合は, 〜させていただく, つきましては — with the words of each grammar point lit up. Optional furigana; keys 1–4 answer, F toggles furigana, S switches to sentence by sentence.
