About

Tokidoki (時々, "now and then") is a free place to practise Japanese a little at a time. It started as verb conjugation drills and grew into practice for JLPT N5 to N3: conjugation, grammar, kana and kanji, vocabulary, graded stories, listening and three easy news stories every day.

Everything works without an account, and spaced repetition brings back what you keep missing instead of what you already know. Log in only if you want your progress on more than one device.

Japanese Practice — JLPT N5 to N3

Tokidoki is a free, no-login collection of Japanese practice exercises for JLPT N5 to N3, in small chunks by level and topic. Every exercise is scheduled with spaced repetition (SRS) so you review what you need most, not what you already know.

Verb conjugation drills

Work through verbs in themed JLPT chunks, covering masu-form, て-form, ta-form, nai-form, tai-form, potential, volitional, passive, causative, ba-conditional, causative-passive, and more — for both godan (u-verb) and ichidan (ru-verb) patterns, plus the irregular verbs する and くる.

Adjective conjugation drills

Practise い-adjective and な-adjective conjugations including present, negative, past, past-negative, te-form, and adverb forms, in themed chunks by JLPT level.

Build Your Own session

Mix and match any combination of verbs, adjectives, and conjugation forms into a custom practice session. Useful for targeting weak spots or reviewing before a test.

Sentence translation practice

Translate full Japanese sentences grouped by JLPT level and grammar point. Toggle between Japanese-to-English and English-to-Japanese modes, with optional furigana readings above kanji.

Bunkei — sentence pattern drill

Pick a few verbs and translate English sentences into Japanese with sentence patterns like 〜させてください (please let me), 〜なければなりません (must) and 〜かもしれません (might), one verb at a time.

Bunkei in the wild — sentence patterns in a story

Read a five-chapter story, N5 to N3, that uses every pattern from the Bunkei drill, and after each sentence choose what its pattern means — 〜てしまう, 〜ておく, 〜わけにはいかない and the rest — with an explanation and a translation.

Graded reading stories

Short stories for JLPT N5, N4 and N3. Tap any word for its reading and meaning and save it as a flashcard, or tap a sentence to see the grammar it uses.

Japanese memes, jokes & pop culture

ダジャレ puns, internet slang like 草 and リア充, anime and manga catchphrases, riddles, comic senryū and rakugo, sorted into JLPT N5, N4 and N3. Tap any word to look it up and save it as a flashcard, tap a sentence for its grammar, and read why each joke is funny.

Fun Japanese phrases by category

What to say when you meet someone, on a first date or on your first day at work, plus famous ad slogans, anime, Ghibli and video-game lines, buzzwords of the year, festival chants, onomatopoeia, otaku words, proverbs and haiku, plus what to say at New Year, birthdays and weddings — each with furigana, English, a note on its origin and audio.

Kuro the Word Detective

A detective story told in Japanese: as Kuro the cat detective's assistant, fill in missing words, question suspects in full sentences, solve なぞなぞ riddles and find the lie in each testimony. Ten cases from JLPT N5 to N3.

JLPT listening practice

438 聴解 questions and fifteen mock tests in the format of the JLPT N5 to N2 listening section, with fifteen recorded voices: hear the question and a short conversation, then pick the right picture — a clock, a calendar, a map, a person — or the right spoken reply. Scripts with furigana and English afterwards, an exam mode with an answer timer, and a review of your mistakes.

Japanese particle quiz

Multiple-choice drills for は, が, を, に, で, へ, と, も, の, and や. Each question shows a sentence with one particle missing plus its English translation; pick the particle that fits out of four choices and get graded instantly. Choose which particles to include so you can target mix-ups like に vs で vs へ, scheduled with spaced repetition so you review the ones you keep missing.

Easy Japanese news, N5 to N3

Short news stories from Japan, each written at JLPT N5, N4 and N3 so you can switch to the level that suits you. Tap any sentence for its translation, words and grammar — news expressions like 〜によると and 〜とみられる included — and save words and grammar points to study later.

Japanese conversation starters

Everyday questions — your weekend, your family, your favourite food, your dreams — to answer in a few sentences of your own. Open hints one step at a time (words, verbs, grammar, a way to begin) and compare with a sample answer written for JLPT N5, N4 or N3.

JLPT word order practice

The 文の組み立て question from the JLPT grammar section: four words and particles are cut out of a sentence, and you put them back in the right order — or, as on the test, pick only the piece that goes in the ★ blank. N5 to N3, with furigana, English and a note on the grammar that fixes the order.

JLPT passage grammar practice

The 文章の文法 question from the JLPT grammar section: read an essay, letter or short story with numbered blanks and choose the word, particle, conjunction or grammar form that fits each one from four choices. Every blank comes with an explanation, plus an English translation of the passage. N5 to N3.

Hiragana & katakana drawing practice

Learn the kana with a hands-on drawing drill: see a romaji prompt, draw the matching hiragana or katakana character by hand, then reveal the answer to check yourself. Covers the full gojuon, dakuten/handakuten, and yōon combinations for both scripts.

JLPT kanji quiz & flashcards

Self-graded kanji flashcards covering JLPT N5 through N1. Test either direction — see the meaning and recall the kanji, or see the kanji and recall its meaning — scheduled with spaced repetition so you review what you find hardest.

Kanji hover reference

Browse the same kanji & meaning pairs as the printable answer-key sheets on screen — kanji and meaning stacked, one side hidden until you hover to peek or click to reveal it for good. Toggle kanji-to-meaning or meaning-to-kanji and filter by JLPT level.

Vocabulary hover reference

Browse JLPT vocabulary (N5–N3) the same way — word and meaning stacked, one side hidden until you hover to peek or click to reveal it for good. Toggle word-to-meaning or meaning-to-word, filter by JLPT level, and choose whether the Japanese side shows kanji, furigana, or the reading spelled out entirely in hiragana/katakana.

Vocabulary quiz by topic

The same JLPT N5–N3 words as a typing quiz, in topic sets of 20–50 words — food, family, time, places, verbs of moving, adjectives and more. Type the meaning or the word, keep your best score per set, and review the words you missed.

Notepad

A Japanese input method for learners: type romaji or an English word, pick the kanji, hiragana or katakana spelling, and see the result with furigana above every kanji.

Words by kanji

A reverse-lookup reference: browse the same JLPT kanji lists (N5–N1) as Kanji Hover, and click any kanji to see every word in the Vocabulary list that contains it, each shown with furigana and its English meaning.

Blog & tips for learning Japanese

Posts about learning Japanese — like which apps, YouTube channels, graded readers, textbooks and teachers help at each level — plus quick guides to verb conjugation, は vs が and JLPT listening, each with a short quiz.

Printable kanji & kana practice sheets

Free printable PDF worksheets for testing recall by hand: JLPT kanji sheets (N5–N1) in both directions — write the kanji from its meaning, or write the meaning from the kanji — plus hiragana and katakana writing sheets. Answer keys included for every sheet.
