Japanese sentence patterns in short stories

Free, no-login practice for Japanese sentence patterns (文型, bunkei) as they turn up in a real text. Six short stories, each in five chapters from JLPT N5 to N3: an exchange student’s year in Japan, a woman who opens a café by the sea, a boy detective looking for the neighbour’s missing cat, a family climbing Mt Fuji, a household robot learning to be part of the family, and a school band getting ready for the culture festival. Every story uses all the patterns from the Bunkei drill: 〜てください, 〜てもいいですか, 〜てはいけません, 〜たことがある, 〜てしまう, 〜ておく, 〜てみる, 〜てくる, 〜つもりだ, 〜かもしれない, 〜ながら, 〜たら, 〜ばよかった, 〜ようになる, 〜てあげる / くれる / もらう, the passive and causative-passive, 〜べきだ, 〜ことにする, 〜ようにする, 〜わけにはいかない, 〜ことはない, 〜てばかりいる, 〜ようとする, 〜ずに and more. After each sentence, with its pattern underlined, choose what the pattern means out of four; then see how it is built and an English translation, with optional furigana.
