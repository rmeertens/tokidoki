Special occasions in Japanese: 特別な日のことば

What to write on the card or say at the party.

  • あけましておめでとうございます！ — Happy New Year! Only from 1 January on. Before midnight you say よいお年を instead. Casual: あけおめ！
  • 今年もよろしくお願いします。 — Please be good to me again this year. Always follows the New Year greeting. Casual: ことよろ！
  • よいお年を！ — Have a good year (ahead)! The last time you see someone before New Year.
  • お誕生日おめでとう！ — Happy birthday! Add ございます to be polite. Kids also sing ハッピーバースデー in English.
  • ご結婚おめでとうございます。 — Congratulations on your marriage. At weddings avoid "cutting" words like 切れる or 別れる, and repeated words like 重ね重ね.
  • 末永くお幸せに。 — May you be happy for ever and ever. The classic line to end a wedding message.
  • ご卒業おめでとうございます。 — Congratulations on graduating. School years end in March, so this is a spring phrase — with cherry blossoms.
  • 合格おめでとう！ — Congrats on passing! For entrance exams — and the JLPT. Students buy キットカット as a lucky charm: きっと勝つ "you'll surely win".
  • 成人おめでとう！ — Congrats on coming of age! Coming-of-Age Day (成人の日), the second Monday of January: young people in kimono and suits.
  • メリークリスマス！ — Merry Christmas! Christmas Eve is a date night in Japan, and the traditional meal is KFC and strawberry shortcake.
  • ハッピーハロウィン！トリック・オア・トリート！ — Happy Halloween! Trick or treat! Mostly a costume party for grown-ups — Shibuya fills with zombies and Marios.
  • これ、義理チョコです。 — This is obligation chocolate. On Valentine's Day women give chocolate: 本命チョコ for the one you love, 義理チョコ for coworkers. Men pay back on White Day, 14 March.
  • お母さん、いつもありがとう。 — Mum, thanks for everything. The standard Mother's Day card, with a red carnation.
  • いつまでも長生きしてね。 — Please live a long, long life. For grandparents on Respect for the Aged Day (敬老の日) in September.
  • お大事に。 — Take care (of yourself). To someone who's ill — doctors and pharmacists say it as you leave.
  • 暑中お見舞い申し上げます。 — Wishing you well in the summer heat. The opening of a summer greeting card, sent in July and August.
  • お年玉ちょうだい！ — Give me my New Year money! Kids get お年玉 — cash in little envelopes — from relatives at New Year.
  • 背が高くなりますように。 — I hope I get taller. A Tanabata wish (7 July), written on a paper strip and tied to bamboo. 〜ますように = "may…".

Story: メアリーの一年 (Mary's year)

一月一日、メアリーは友達にメッセージを送りました。 (On 1 January, Mary sent her friends a message.) メアリー: あけましておめでとうございます！今年もよろしくお願いします。 (Happy New Year! Please be good to me again this year.) 二月には、会社のみんなに義理チョコをあげました。 (In February she gave obligation chocolate to everyone at work.) 七月、七夕の短冊にこう書きました。 (In July she wrote this on her Tanabata wish strip:) メアリー: 日本語が上手になりますように。 (May my Japanese get better.) 十二月、メアリーの誕生日に、友達がケーキを持ってきました。 (In December, on Mary's birthday, her friends brought a cake.) みんな: お誕生日おめでとう！ (Happy birthday!) 帰るとき、メアリーは手を振りました。 (As they went home, Mary waved.) メアリー: よいお年を！ (Have a good New Year!)
