Famous quotes from history in Japanese: 歴史の名言

Lines from warlords, thinkers and teachers that are still quoted today.

  • 鳴かぬなら殺してしまえホトトギス — If the cuckoo won't sing, kill it. Oda Nobunaga's character in a famous trio of verses — ruthless.
  • 鳴かぬなら鳴かせてみようホトトギス — If the cuckoo won't sing, make it sing. Toyotomi Hideyoshi — clever and resourceful.
  • 鳴かぬなら鳴くまで待とうホトトギス — If the cuckoo won't sing, wait till it does. Tokugawa Ieyasu — patient. And it was Ieyasu who ended up ruling Japan.
  • 少年よ、大志を抱け。 — Boys, be ambitious. William S. Clark, leaving his school in Sapporo in 1877. There's a statue of him pointing.
  • 天は人の上に人を造らず、人の下に人を造らず。 — Heaven creates no one above or below anyone else. Fukuzawa Yukichi, 1872 — the man on the old 10,000-yen note.
  • 和を以て貴しとなす。 — Harmony is to be valued. The first article of Prince Shotoku's Constitution, 604 — the root of Japan's love of 和.
  • 為せば成る、為さねば成らぬ何事も。 — Try and it can be done; don't, and nothing will be. Uesugi Yozan, 18th-century lord. A school-motto classic.
  • 芸術は爆発だ！ — Art is an explosion! Artist Taro Okamoto in a 1981 TV ad. He made the Tower of the Sun in Osaka.

Story: 歴史の授業 (History class)

先生: ホトトギスが鳴かないとき、みんなはどうしますか？ (If a cuckoo won't sing, what would you do?) タロウ: 鳴かぬなら鳴くまで待とうホトトギス。ぼくは待ちます。 (If it won't sing, wait till it does. I'd wait.) ユキ: 鳴かぬなら鳴かせてみようホトトギス。歌を教えます！ (If it won't sing, make it sing. I'd teach it a song!) ケン: うーん…スマホで鳴き声を流します。 (Hmm… I'd play a recording on my phone.) 先生: …新しいですね。 (…That's a new one.) 授業の最後に、先生は黒板に書きました。 (At the end of class, the teacher wrote on the blackboard:) 先生: 少年よ、大志を抱け。 (Boys, be ambitious.) ケン: 先生、ぼくの大志は、昼ごはんです。 (Sir, my ambition is lunch.)
