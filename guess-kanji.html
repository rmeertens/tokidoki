GTK - Easy Word Order

    What this page is for

    The goal: more words, less memorising. Most Japanese words are built from a few thousand kanji, and a kanji usually keeps its reading from word to word: 電 is でん in 電車 (train) and in 電気 (electricity); 気 is き in 天気 (weather) and in 電気. Learn 電車 and 天気 first and you can work out 電気 the first time you see it — reading and meaning — without ever studying it. A word you get right that way is a word you didn’t have to memorise.

    The catch is the order. In a normal list (by JLPT level, by frequency, alphabetically) the word that would give a kanji away usually comes long after, or long before, the words that use it. This page re-orders the JLPT N5–N3 vocabulary so they come close together:

    1. Meet — a plain word brings in a new kanji, one new kanji at a time, read the way it’s read most often.
    2. Guess — soon after come the words made only of kanji you’ve met, marked 🎯. Before you show the answer, say the reading and the meaning out loud or in your head. Stuck? Show hint gives the kanji you may know, and Words you’ve seen the words you met them in; H opens them in turn. A word whose kanji is read more than one way on its own (中: なか, ちゅう) says so.
    3. Grade honestly — Got it only if you had both right. Every word, guessed or not, then comes back for review with spaced repetition.

    The order is a greedy search, worked out in your browser each time you start: of the words left, it takes the one you can guess right now, or else the word that sets up the most guessable words while bringing in the fewest new kanji. It plans from what you’ve actually studied: a kanji counts as known if you met it in the last 40 new words, or in a word you know well. In JLPT list order, about 6 of the first 100 words can be guessed when they arrive; on this path, over 60.

    Whether a word can be guessed is judged by simple rules: its kanji’s readings must be the usual ones, and at least half its kanji must show in its English meaning. Some 🎯 words will still surprise you — that’s fine, they’re reviewed like any other card.

    Where the idea comes from

    This page is built on BlaiseLabs’ work: the idea, the greedy path and the way a word is split into its kanji and readings all come from GuessWithAnki, an Anki add-on that re-orders your own deck this way (and its Japanese-only predecessor, GuessWithKanji). Their video explains it, with results for Japanese, Chinese, English and medical vocabulary — well worth nine minutes — and so does their write-up.

    GTK - Easy Word Order — JLPT words in an order you can guess

    Most Japanese words are built from kanji you will meet again and again, read the same way: 電 is でん in 電車 (train) and in 電気 (electricity), 気 is き in 天気 (weather) and in 電気. GTK - Easy Word Order puts the JLPT N5, N4 and N3 vocabulary in an order where each kanji arrives in a plain word first, so the words built from it can be guessed — reading and meaning — on first sight instead of memorised. The order is a greedy path, after GuessWithAnki, planned again in your browser from the words you have already studied, and every word is reviewed with spaced repetition.
